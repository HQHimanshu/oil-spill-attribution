# IMO MARPOL 73/78 Annex I - Maritime Discharge Regulations & Evidentiary Standards

**Source**: International Maritime Organization (IMO)  
**Title**: International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships (MARPOL) - Annex I: Prevention of Pollution by Oil  
**Date**: 2023-11-01  
**Document Type**: International Regulatory Standard  
**URL**: https://www.imo.org/en/KnowledgeCentre/Conventions/Pages/MARPOL.aspx  

## 1. Operational Discharge Limitations
Under MARPOL Annex I Regulation 15 and Regulation 34:
* Any discharge into the sea of oil or oily mixtures from ships is prohibited except when all the following conditions are satisfied:
  1. The ship is proceeding en route;
  2. The oily mixture is processed through an approved Oil Discharge Monitoring and Control System (ODMCS) / Oily Water Separator (OWS);
  3. The oil content of the effluent without dilution does not exceed 15 parts per million (15 ppm);
  4. The discharge is located outside defined "Special Areas" (e.g., Mediterranean Sea, Baltic Sea, Red Sea, Gulfs area) where discharge standards are strictly zero for cargo spaces.

## 2. Investigative Evidence Requirements
Maritime attribution of illegal discharges relies on a chain of technical evidence:
* **Spatial and Temporal Coincidence**: Establishing that a vessel was within the reconstructed origin window when the illegal discharge occurred.
* **Corroborating Drift Analysis**: Demonstrating that hydrodynamic drift vectors link the observed slick directly back to the vessel's tracked voyage.
* **Vessel Operational Profile**: High-risk profiles include machinery bilge discharges, tank cleaning washings, and oily ballast disposal en route.
* **Decision Support Disclaimer**: Satellite and AIS analytical attribution constitutes intelligence decision support; physical boarding, Oil Record Book (ORB) inspection, and gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) chemical oil fingerprinting are necessary for legal prosecution.
